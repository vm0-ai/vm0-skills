## Style characteristics

- **Composition:** the hero sits in a central band, occasionally offset, and overlaps the title. Tall settled products can occupy roughly three-quarters to nearly the full height. Broad titles span much of the width, usually over two lines; small interface details stay peripheral. Match visual mass for a round or wide product instead of stretching it to a tall ratio.
- **Material:** photoreal-looking product surfaces and molded CGI ornament coexist with completely flat graphic masks. Glossy, soft, granular, translucent and fuzzy surfaces create tactile contrast. The production method cannot be inferred from pixels alone.
- **Palette:** cream frequently ties the worlds together. Black/cream, green/cream, caramel/cream, pink/red, brown/cream and teal/yellow are observed combinations, not a required sequence. Use a dominant field, strong type contrast and a few derived accents.
- **Typography:** the visible headlines are heavy, broad geometric/grotesk capitals with mostly regular widths, solid fills and occasional fine outlines. They are not predominantly inflated bubble letters or tall condensed letters. Exact font identification is not established. Small labels use compact bold sans.
- **Depth and light:** controlled studio highlights reveal product volume; large objects may be cropped by frame edges. Shallow layered depth and overlap carry the composition, without a furnished room or moving cinema camera.
- **Motion:** product scale changes and downward/upward replacements are stronger than ambient motion. A round expanding mask can carry a new field and title while old content remains outside it. Foreground motifs arrive and settle on staggered timings, then continue light drift or rotation.
- **Rhythm:** most intermediate worlds receive about 1.5-2 seconds. The final teal world lasts considerably longer, with local movement and a late CTA. The exact count and seconds are properties of this example.
- **Mood:** exuberant, tactile, playful product advertising with confident editorial scale. Y2K energy comes from materials, checkerboards, coils, stars and mixed graphic layers, not from adding a pointer to every shot.

## Style invariants and optional content

Keep the central material-rich hero, broad type behind it, shallow mixed-media depth, coordinated color worlds and responsive scale/mask transitions.

The source does contain a conspicuous black/white mouse arrow and a hover ring near its selection row. These are interface demonstration devices, not the style's identity. For style-only briefs, omit the pointer. If the user requests interaction, keep the pointer small and subordinate as specified in the skill. The 2-3% pointer limit is a correction based on user feedback, not a measurement of this source.

The ice creams, source brand, six names, navigation labels, selector numbering, exact palette order and CTA copy are replaceable content. Keep source watermarks and accidental label/number inconsistencies out of new work.

## Transfer example

For a lamp collection, translate cream/brown/teal worlds into the brand palette; use glass, metal and ceramic product surfaces; derive rings, tubes, ribbed shapes and light discs from the lamps. Place broad two-line names behind one large lamp. Reuse the same stage while masks and scale changes reveal each variant. Add a selection row or pointer only when the brief calls for an interactive catalog.

## Recognition check

At a still frame, the material-rich product, oversized broad lettering and cropped graphic world should identify the style. Across a transition, those layers should change together. A giant arrow, stretched bubble word, plain product on an empty backdrop or small products in equal cards does not substitute for that combination.
