## Style characteristics

- **Surface:** pale warm uncoated-paper appearance with fine speckles and grain. Photographs, product cutouts, flat ink-like lettering and evidence windows share that substrate. Roughness is restrained; it does not require torn edges or adhesive tape.
- **Palette:** ivory, black and gold/ochre dominate much of the film. Red expands in the dense middle; mint/green, red, gray and dark objects create a later color row. The accents have unequal areas and enter at different times, rather than filling every scene equally.
- **Image language:** metallic/gold product cutouts, glossy black vinyl, laptop edges and colored rectangular objects retain photographic highlights and some perspective. Monochrome portraits, silhouettes and car imagery provide another tonal register; black waveform windows and repeated angular marks provide graphic evidence. A uniform flat-illustration treatment would erase this material contrast.
- **Composition:** the full canvas faces the viewer, but local regions can form uneven panels, split, disappear or turn into rows. Much of the structure comes from adjacent image/color edges rather than a border drawn around every item. Headline and objects can cross panel boundaries.
- **Typography:** normal-width bold grotesk capitals form large readable horizontal phrases. Fine outline/solid handoffs animate those phrases. Enormous cropped black glyphs are a separate, temporary structural layer; not every phrase fills the frame vertically. Small supporting labels complete the hierarchy. No exact font is established.
- **Depth:** shallow compositing, clipped photo windows, occlusion and varied object scales create depth. Real highlights and object perspective remain visible. Local product rotation is compatible with the style even though the master canvas does not orbit or dolly.
- **Motion:** records roll/turn and grow; products tilt, rotate and repeat in staggered instances; crop windows slide; evidence rows align; type switches between outline and solid. A dominant carrier and several smaller changes often happen at once. Continuous movement inside image windows can coexist with cutout motion.
- **Rhythm and mood:** brisk editorial assembly, with major reorganizations roughly every 1-2 seconds and smaller activity between them. The end board settles progressively rather than becoming an early frozen title card. The effect is cultured, technical and tactile, like a designed publication continually reformatting itself.

## Style invariants and optional content

Keep a continuous paper canvas, mixed photographic/graphic evidence, repeatable anchors, changing local layout, distinct type scales and concurrent motion that resolves into a collected board.

Source laptops, records, gold devices, books, portraits, car imagery, waveform shapes and product claims are replaceable evidence. Choose several material categories appropriate to the user's topic. Do not insert a record or a portrait solely because it appeared in the reference.

The seven functional beats in the skill are a production interpretation of this particular pacing. They do not prove that every editorial collage needs seven phrases, the same props or the exact source seconds. The bundled guide demonstrates timing and layout; its generic shapes are not an art-direction reference.

## Transfer example

For a research publication, combine colored document/book cutouts, monochrome field photographs, a scan or data window and diagram fragments on warm paper. Keep a few recognizable objects while panels change scale, a circular object carries a title transition, and evidence gathers into a final archive row. Use the user's own claims and palette accents.

## Recognition check

At a still frame, the image should combine photographic material, ink-like type and editorial hierarchy. Across a transition, recognizable evidence should move and re-layout in parallel. A tidy equal-card grid, identical illustrated stickers, uniformly matte objects, or a lone oversized word replacing every scene would miss the source's material and compositional contrasts.
